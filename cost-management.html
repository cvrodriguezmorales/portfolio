Cost Management

C. Rodríguez Morales July 12, 2026
Welcome to this section of my portfolio! In my journey studying Industrial Engineering at the University of Puerto Rico, Mayagüez Campus, one of the most pivotal courses I have completed is ININ 4087: Cost Management.

While engineering often focuses on the physical optimization of systems, this class reinforced that true process optimization requires a deep understanding of financial strategy. Whether evaluating the efficiency of a vehicle assembly line, managing project budgets for a collaborative team, or analyzing a supply chain, grasping how costs behave is an essential skill for any engineer.

Here is a look at the core competencies and strategic insights I developed through this course and its culminating final project.

The Core of Cost Management: The foundation of the curriculum centered on the mechanics of financial tracking and control. We learned to identify, calculate, and analyze various types of costs, including indirect, direct, variable, and fixed.


Beyond basic accounting, the course emphasized modern cost analysis techniques crucial for continuous improvement, such as:

  • Activity-Based Costing (ABC) & Life-Cycle Costing: Methods to accurately assign costs to specific processes and evaluate the financial impact of a product from conception to disposal.
  • Target Costing & Value Engineering: Proactive approaches to designing products that meet customer expectations while remaining strictly within profitable cost boundaries.
  • Root Cause Evaluation: The analytical framework needed to investigate the causes of high costs or reduced profits in manufacturing and service environments, allowing us to design better cost management systems.

Navigating Market Structures: An eye-opening aspect of the course was exploring how external market structures dictate internal financial strategies. We analyzed theoretical models like perfect competition, where a massive amount of participants means no single entity influences the market. In this utopian scenario, companies must maximize profits strictly through internal cost reduction.


Conversely, we examined imperfect structures like monopolies and oligopolies, where a smaller number of players control pricing, availability, and competitive access. Recognizing these market dynamics is critical when forecasting the financial viability of any new engineering project.

Strategic Pricing Policies: For our final project, my team and I synthesized these concepts to analyze different pricing policies and their alignment with industry needs. The policy a company chooses must perfectly align with its product nature, cost structure, and competitive sector. We explored four primary strategies:


  • Cost-Based Pricing: This strategy calculates a baseline price to cover production and ensure a consistent margin, utilizing the break-even formula (Fixed Costs / Unit Contribution Margin). It is highly effective for traditional manufacturing and regulated industries where stable, transparent costs are necessary.
  • Value-Based Pricing: Instead of focusing strictly on internal costs, this approach determines price based on the customer's perceived value. It allows for premium pricing and is ideal for highly differentiated, innovative products like luxury goods or cutting-edge technology.
  • Demand-Based Pricing: This dynamic model adjusts prices based on market segmentation and consumer price elasticity. It maximizes revenue in environments with fluctuating demand and limited capacity, heavily utilized by airlines and ride-sharing platforms (e.g., surge pricing).
  • Competition-Based Pricing: In saturated markets with highly similar products, prices are set by benchmarking direct rivals. Strategies range from aggressive penetration pricing to price matching, helping companies maintain their market share without triggering prolonged price wars.

 

Ultimately, ININ 4087 bridged the gap between tactical resource usage and high-level profitability analysis. Presenting these strategic pricing recommendations in our final executive report underscored the importance of clearly communicating complex financial data to decision-makers. As I continue to grow as an industrial engineer, the ability to seamlessly align production costs with market realities will remain a cornerstone of my approach to system optimization.
