Deterministic Models in Operations Research

C. Rodríguez Morales July 12, 2026

I took this course in the spring of 2026, and it taught me how to turn a messy real-world decision into a mathematical model and find the best possible answer. I learned to recognize, formulate, and solve linear optimization problems, from deciding how much of each product to make to routing shipments through a network, and, just as importantly, how to interpret the results and turn them into practical recommendations.

  • The technical skills gained: 
    • Linear Programming Formulation - Translating real-world problems such as production, blending, diet, staff scheduling, and capital budgeting into decision variables, an objective function, and constraints.
    • Graphical and Simplex Methods - Solving LP problems graphically and with the simplex algorithm (including the Big-M method), and identifying special cases such as infeasible, unbounded, and alternative optimal solutions.
    • Duality and Sensitivity Analysis - Using the dual problem and sensitivity reports to understand how much each resource is worth and how far costs or capacities can change before the optimal solution changes.
    • Computer-Based Optimization - Building and solving LP models in Excel Solver and interpreting its output.
    • Transportation, Assignment, and Transshipment Problems - Finding the lowest-cost way to ship goods from supply points to demand points, and assigning workers to tasks optimally with the Hungarian method.
    • Network Optimization - Solving shortest path problems with Dijkstra's algorithm, as well as maximum flow and minimum spanning tree problems.
    • Project Management - Scheduling projects and identifying their critical activities with the Critical Path Method (CPM) and PERT.
